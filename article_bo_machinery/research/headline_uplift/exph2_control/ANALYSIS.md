## New machinery finding (feeds H1-MASK)

optuna-gp on d3_L5 exhausts the **entire 400-ask horizon producing only
~30 unique configurations** (median 30/400; H1's 27 unique at 80 asks
barely grows at 5× the proposals) and ~55/400 on d5_L5 — while on the
huge d6 space it needs just 85 asks for 80 unique. GPSampler's proposal
generator *saturates* on small categorical spaces: the duplicates are not
occasional collisions but the generator's steady state. "More budget"
cannot fix it; only machinery (dedup/generator) change can. This is the
strongest single mechanism datum in the audit so far.

## Practical readings

- Equal-proposal budgets (what every benchmark table implicitly uses)
  and equal-unique-evaluation budgets **disagree about who wins** on 2 of
  4 benchmarks. On expensive objectives — where BO is used — the unique
  count is what costs money; leaderboards charge the wrong meter.
- The refund is heterogeneous: optuna-tpe converts refunded budget into
  solves; hyperopt-tpe mostly does not. An audit must measure both waste
  AND what the waste displaces.

## Threats & limitations

- The memoization equivalence requires deterministic objectives (all four
  here are; pest is our disclosed determinized variant).
- optuna-gp's pest cell carries over from H1 (0 revisits there — a re-run
  would be a no-op by the equivalence argument; stated in every table).
- Ranking metric is median final best with 1e-9 tie tolerance among the 6
  audited libraries, fixed in DESIGN before the run; other metrics (mean,
  solve-rate-first) were not evaluated and Z under them is unknown.
- Same benchmark-structure caveats as H1 (cat-ackley additivity).
- d3_L5 optuna-tpe/optuna-gp/hyperopt hit the 400-ask horizon at 46/30/67
  unique — H2's d3 numbers are horizon-limited for these libraries
  (disclosed in the table via uniq/prop; the ranking there was unchanged
  anyway).
