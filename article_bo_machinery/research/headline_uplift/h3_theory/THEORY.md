## Setting and notation

A pool-based BO loop over a finite search space: at step t a generator G
produces a candidate pool P_t = {x_{t,1}, …, x_{t,n_t}} (possibly
conditioned on the evaluation history H_{t−1}), a selector π picks one
point x_t ∈ P_t, the objective f is evaluated there, and
B_T(π) = min_{t ≤ T} f(x_t) is the best-so-far at budget T. The *oracle
selector* π* picks x_t ∈ argmin_{x ∈ P_t} f(x). All objectives here are
deterministic.

## Lemma A (oracle domination on exogenous pools — a theorem)

**Claim.** Suppose the pool sequence (P_t)_{t≤T} is *exogenous*: it is
generated by any deterministic or random process that does not depend on
the selector's choices (e.g. fixed LHS pools, any pre-drawn sequence, or
pools driven by an independent random stream). Couple all selectors to
the same realized pools. Then for every selection policy π (including
randomized and history-dependent ones) and every t ≤ T, pathwise:

  B_t(π*) ≤ B_t(π).

**Proof.** On the coupled realization both policies see the identical
pools P_1, …, P_t. For each s ≤ t, π picks some x_s ∈ P_s, so
f(x_s) ≥ min_{x∈P_s} f(x) = f(x*_s), the oracle's pick. Taking minima
over s ≤ t preserves the pointwise inequality:
B_t(π) = min_s f(x_s) ≥ min_s f(x*_s) = B_t(π*). ∎

**Remarks.** (i) The statement is pathwise and holds at every budget, so
it also bounds means, medians, and any monotone functional of the
best-so-far curve. (ii) The proof uses only that every selector is
restricted to the same pools — this is exactly what the audit's
"replace the acquisition with −f" construction implements when the
generator stream is seeded independently of selections. (iii) The lemma
is silent about *adaptive* generators; that is Proposition B's territory,
and the distinction is the honest boundary of what the audit proves.

## Proposition B (adaptive pools — explicitly a conjecture)

With a history-dependent generator (ours conditions on the incumbent:
local Gaussian moves around the best-so-far), different selectors induce
*different* pool sequences, and Lemma A's coupling breaks: in principle a
weak selector could steer the generator into luckier regions. We
conjecture the domination survives our generator class:

**Conjecture.** Let G be a generator whose pool distribution depends on
history only through the incumbent x̂_t and satisfies: (i) a global
component with full support independent of history (our LHS half), and
(ii) a local component whose distribution is continuous in x̂_t. Then for
every policy π, E[B_T(π*)] ≤ E[B_T(π)] + ε(G), where ε(G) vanishes as
the global component's share grows.

**Status.** Not proved here; stated so the paper cannot silently upgrade
the audit into a theorem it isn't. Empirical record consistent with
ε ≈ 0 at our scales (corrected and re-verified at review): matching
E3's GP-EI/RF-EI/TPE/Random surrogate arms per-seed against E2's
oracle + keep + combination-dedup arm (same seeds and objectives,
n_cand = 1000, budgets 10/40/80) gives **0 of 2250 matched comparisons**
in which a surrogate beat the oracle. (E2 itself is surrogate-free by
design — its arms are generator/dedup variants under the oracle
acquisition; the first draft mis-cited it and was corrected on review.)
A counterexample generator showing why assumption (i) is needed: make
the generator purely local — every pool is n Gaussian jitters of the
current incumbent, no global half. The oracle then locks into the first
basin its incumbent reaches (each pool concentrates ever closer to the
local minimum, a fixed point), while a policy that deliberately picks a
locally-suboptimal pool point toward a deeper distant basin drags all
future pools there and, for large enough budget, achieves a strictly
better expected best-so-far. Unconditional domination fails exactly
because the global-support component is absent.

## Proposition C (guidance-dial decay law)

Fix a pool of n values with a unique minimum, f_1 < f_2 ≤ … ≤ f_n
(ties among non-minimal values are allowed and harmless), and gaps
Δ_j = f_j − f_1 > 0 (j ≥ 2). The dial selects
argmax_i { −f_i + σ ε_i } with iid noise ε_i and dial σ ≥ 0.

**(a) Gaussian monotonicity.** With ε_i ~ N(0,1) iid, let
p(σ) = P(the pool minimizer f_1 is selected). Then

  p(σ) = E_e [ Π_{j≥2} Φ(e + Δ_j/σ) ],  e ~ N(0,1),

p is continuous on (0,∞), lim_{σ→0⁺} p = 1, lim_{σ→∞} p = 1/n, and p is
**monotone non-increasing in σ**.

*Proof.* The winning event {−f_1 + σε_1 > −f_j + σε_j ∀j} is
{ε_j < ε_1 + Δ_j/σ ∀j}; conditioning on ε_1 = e and using independence
gives the product formula (ties have probability zero). Each factor
Φ(e + Δ_j/σ) is non-increasing in σ for every fixed e because Δ_j > 0
and Φ is increasing; hence the integrand, and so p, is non-increasing.
Limits: as σ→0⁺ each factor → 1 for every e (Δ_j/σ → ∞) and dominated
convergence gives p → 1; as σ→∞ the gaps vanish and p → P(ε_1 is the
maximum of n iid continuous draws) = 1/n. Continuity is dominated
convergence in σ. ∎

**(b) Gumbel twin (closed form).** With ε_i iid standard Gumbel and the
same selection rule, P(i selected) = exp(−f_i/σ) / Σ_j exp(−f_j/σ) —
the Plackett–Luce/softmax law with temperature σ — by the standard
Gumbel-max property. Same limits (indicator of the argmin as σ→0⁺;
uniform as σ→∞) and the same monotonicity for p(σ) (its logit is
−log Σ_{j≥2} exp(−Δ_j/σ) …, decreasing since each Δ_j/σ decreases).
Our experiments used **Gaussian** noise; (b) is stated because it makes
the selection law exactly softmax, which is the form theory-adjacent
readers will recognize — not because it was run.

**(c) Sequential reading and the correct far-end baseline.** In the
loop, σ interpolates between the oracle selector (σ = 0) and, as σ→∞,
*uniform selection over the same generator's pools* — not free random
search over the space. ("Pools" here means the post-dedup-mask
candidate set: run_bo masks candidates duplicating already-evaluated
combinations before selection, identically at every σ, so the masked
set is what any selector — noisy or not — chooses from.) Per-step selection quality degrades monotonically
by (a); we do **not** claim a theorem that the *final* best-at-budget of
the adaptive sequential loop is monotone in σ (pools are adaptive;
Proposition B's caveat applies, and between-generator statistics such as
E8's W(σ=100) residual are outside (a)'s scope entirely). The decay-law
*fit* below is therefore a descriptive model of the empirical medians,
with (a) supplying the shape constraints (two pinned limits, monotone
interior), not a derived formula.

## Fitted decay law (protocol per DESIGN.md; numbers in ANALYSIS.md)

One idealization to note: Prop C(a)'s unique-minimum/distinct-gaps
premise is almost-surely true of the real pools (continuous LHS +
Gaussian halves) but is not an enforced invariant — exact ties are
possible in principle at the [0,1] clip boundary; the proposition
holds for its stated setting, and the loop satisfies that setting
almost surely, not exactly.

m(σ) = m₀ + (m∞ − m₀)/(1 + (s/σ)^γ): m₀ pinned at the σ=0 median (the
oracle end), m∞ the fitted plateau (the uniform-over-pool end), s the
half-loss dial value, γ the sharpness. Fit on E8 medians; scored held-out
on E7 medians (same grid, disjoint seeds, no refitting).
