\title[The Machinery Confound in Mixed-Space Bayesian Optimization]{The
Machinery Confound: Acquisition-Optimization Machinery Can Dominate Surrogate
Comparisons in Mixed and Categorical Bayesian Optimization}

\begin{abstract}
Comparisons of Bayesian-optimization (BO) methods over mixed and categorical
search spaces are usually read as statements about surrogate models. We show
that they are frequently statements about the surrounding \emph{machinery}
instead --- the candidate generation the acquisition is optimized over, and
the duplicate handling that decides which proposals are admissible --- and
that the effect is live in the deployed ecosystem, not just in principle.
Auditing six widely used BO libraries and a random-sampling control at their
documented defaults with a surrogate-free objective wrapper (1{,}050 runs
across the seven arms; six benchmarks, 25 seeds, budget 80), we find that
three of the six silently re-spend at least 10\% of the evaluation budget on
already-evaluated categorical combinations beyond what unavoidable collisions
explain, two exceed 25\%, and one exceeds 40\% --- a median of 53 of 80
evaluations ground into revisits, touching only ${\sim}27$ unique
configurations, by the library that simultaneously posts the matrix's
\emph{best} categorical solve record: convergence curves are blind to the
waste. One library instead terminates early rather than propose a duplicate.
When we equalize duplicate handling --- charging each library for unique
evaluations only, which by construction leaves sampler trajectories
bit-identical on deterministic objectives --- the apparent library ranking
changes on three of four benchmarks; one sampler's apparent weakness on small
categorical spaces (7 of 25 solves) is revealed as mostly machinery, not
model (22 of 25 once duplicates are refunded).
A controlled study of one research pipeline then isolates the mechanisms.
Under an \emph{oracle-ceiling audit} that replaces every acquisition score
with the true objective, a single degree of freedom in local candidate
generation caps what perfect guidance can attain (restricted loses to
permissive on 25 of 25 paired seeds, $p = 6.0\times10^{-8}$, worsening by
factors of 8 to 14 as the pool shrinks), and encoding-level duplicate
detection re-spends a median of 78 of 80 oracle picks. We prove the audit is
a genuine ceiling for exogenous candidate pools, state the adaptive case as
an explicit conjecture, and derive a monotone decay law for selection quality
under noisy guidance. We release \texttt{bo-audit}, the objective wrapper
behind the ecosystem audit, together with a disclosure checklist for
machinery-controlled comparisons: shared schema-aware proposals,
combination-level deduplication, paired seeds, per-seed win statistics, and
reported excess-over-collision revisit counts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

% Argument: (1) BO quality is usually attributed to the surrogate; (2) in
% mixed/categorical spaces the acquisition cannot be optimized in closed form,
% so every method ships bespoke search machinery; (3) that machinery is rarely
% shared, audited, or even fully described; (4) we show it can dominate
% outcomes; (5) contributions.

The sample efficiency of Bayesian optimization is conventionally credited to
its surrogate model, and comparative studies of surrogates --- Gaussian
processes, random forests, density-ratio methods, spline models --- are read
as evidence about which model class best captures the objective
\cite{Snoek_Larochelle_Adams_2012, bergstra2011algorithms}. Over continuous
domains this attribution is at least plausible: the acquisition function can
be optimized to near-optimality with standard multi-start gradient methods, so
the surrogate is the main thing that differs between pipelines. Over mixed and
categorical domains the situation is different. The acquisition must be
optimized over a discrete or hybrid space, every method ships its own
machinery for doing so --- candidate pools, local search, trust regions,
bandit layers \cite{hutter2011sequential, ru2020cocabo, wan2021think} --- and
that machinery is typically inseparable from the method being evaluated.

How much does this matter outside carefully constructed examples? We measured
it in the deployed ecosystem. Wrapping the objective function itself --- a
vantage point every optimizer must pass through, requiring no access to
library internals --- we audited six widely used BO libraries, plus a
random-sampling control, at their documented defaults on six categorical and
mixed benchmarks (25 seeds each, budget 80; 1{,}050 runs across the seven
arms). Three of the six re-spend at least 10\% of the
evaluation budget on already-evaluated categorical combinations \emph{beyond}
the collisions unavoidable at that budget; two exceed 25\%, one exceeds
40\%. The extreme case evaluates a median of only ${\sim}27$ unique
configurations out of 80 charged evaluations on a 125-combination space ---
while posting the matrix's best categorical solve record, so no convergence
curve gives it away. On a $5^{25}$-combination space, where a uniform random
sampler essentially never collides, one default sampler still re-spends a
median of 16 evaluations in 80. At the other extreme, one library's runhistory
machinery terminates the run early rather than propose a duplicate. These are
not exotic corner cases: the duplicate-suggestion behavior of both audited
TPE implementations is acknowledged in their libraries' own issue trackers,
and the audit turns it --- and the behaviors with no paper trail at all ---
into numbers.

The waste is not an automatic verdict on quality --- that is precisely the
confound. When we re-ran the wasteful libraries charging only \emph{unique}
evaluations (a memoization control that leaves sampler trajectories
bit-identical on deterministic objectives), the apparent ranking of the six
libraries changed on three of four benchmarks: the sampler that looked
weakest on small categorical spaces recovered from 7 of 25 solves to 22 of
25. Benchmark tables implicitly charge proposals; expensive objectives charge
unique evaluations; the two meters disagree about who wins.

This paper isolates the mechanisms behind such effects in a controlled
pipeline, through two concrete failure modes. The first lives in candidate
generation: a single degree of freedom in the local proposal distribution ---
whether a candidate may keep the incumbent's categorical combination while
refining its continuous coordinates --- determines whether \emph{any}
acquisition, however well informed, can exploit on mixed spaces. The second
lives in duplicate handling: comparing raw encodings rather than decoded
combinations lets a pipeline silently re-spend most of a categorical
budget re-measuring points it has already evaluated, with no visible
trace in a convergence curve. Both are ordinary-looking implementation
choices; neither is reported in a typical experimental section --- and both
have live counterparts in the audited libraries.

Our instrument for separating these effects from the surrogate is an
\emph{oracle-ceiling audit}: run the unchanged loop with the acquisition
score replaced by the true objective, so that the machinery is exercised
under perfect guidance. Auditing each machinery variant this way costs
minutes and no surrogate fits, and it turns ``the method underperformed''
into an attributable statement --- either the machinery's ceiling was the
binding constraint, or the surrogate failed short of a ceiling that
permitted more. The spline surrogate whose puzzling failure motivated
this work serves here as a case study: the general mechanisms are
established on the oracle and on other surrogate families, and applying
the audit and repairing both failure modes did not rescue it --- it did
something more useful, converting an unattributable mixed-space failure
into a clean negative result about the model itself. The two defects contaminate
comparisons in different ways: the duplicate leak taxes every method
family we tested by a comparable margin, while the generator restriction
binds in proportion to how close a method runs to the machinery's
ceiling --- which is precisely why attributing anything to a surrogate
requires knowing where that ceiling is.

This paper makes five contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{An in-the-wild machinery audit of six deployed BO
  libraries.} A surrogate-free objective wrapper (\texttt{bo-audit},
  released) counts decoded-combination revisits uniformly across
  libraries at their documented defaults. Headline: 3 of 6 libraries
  waste ${\ge}10\%$ of budget beyond unavoidable collisions on at least
  one benchmark (2 of 6 ${\ge}25\%$; 1 of 6 ${\ge}40\%$), the heaviest
  waster simultaneously posts the matrix's best categorical solve record, and one
  library terminates early rather than duplicate --- all invisible to
  convergence curves.
  \item \textbf{A budget-equalized re-comparison.} Charging unique
  evaluations only (trajectory-preserving memoization on deterministic
  objectives), the apparent library ranking changes on 3 of 4
  benchmarks under a pre-registered ranking metric; the refund converts
  into solves for one TPE implementation and not the other, so waste is
  necessary but not sufficient for a ranking artifact.
  \item \textbf{The oracle-ceiling audit, with its exact scope.}
  Replacing the acquisition score with the true objective turns the BO
  loop into oracle-guided search. We prove the resulting bound is a
  theorem for exogenous candidate pools, exhibit a counterexample
  generator showing why the adaptive case needs an assumption, state
  that case as an explicit conjecture --- supported by 0 violations in
  2{,}250 matched surrogate-versus-oracle comparisons --- and derive a
  monotone decay law for selection quality under noisy guidance.
  \item \textbf{Two demonstrated failure modes.} A restricted local
  proposal distribution (all local candidates forced to change the
  incumbent's categorical combination) loses to its permissive
  counterpart on every paired seed of the CoCaBO mixed benchmarks under
  the oracle, with a shortfall that grows as the candidate pool shrinks;
  encoding-level duplicate detection lets a majority of the budget be
  re-spent on known categorical combinations across every method family
  we tested --- the controlled twins of the behaviors measured in the
  wild.
  \item \textbf{A machinery-controlled comparison protocol.} Shared
  schema-aware candidate generation, combination-level deduplication,
  paired per-seed designs, win/tie/loss reporting with signed-rank
  tests, and disclosed excess-over-collision revisit counts. Repairing
  and sharing the machinery did not flip the motivating surrogate's
  ranking --- it made the ranking \emph{attributable}, localizing a
  mixed-space failure to the surrogate itself while two other methods
  won through the identical loop.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
% =============================================================================

% Position: we do not propose new machinery; the machinery pieces are standard.
% The contribution is the audit + the demonstration that machinery choices
% confound surrogate attribution, which the literature largely leaves implicit.

\paragraph{Mixed and categorical BO.} SMAC optimizes a random-forest
surrogate's acquisition by local search over configuration neighborhoods
(one-exchange categorical flips) interleaved with random candidates
\cite{hutter2011sequential}. TPE models densities over good and bad trials and
handles categoricals natively \cite{bergstra2011algorithms, watanabe2023tree,
akiba2019optuna}. CoCaBO couples a multi-armed bandit over categorical
combinations with a GP over the continuous subspace \cite{ru2020cocabo};
Casmopolitan builds Hamming-distance trust regions around the incumbent
\cite{wan2021think}; COMBO works over combinatorial graphs \cite{oh2019combo}.
For GPs specifically, the baseline treatment of categorical inputs is a
continuous relaxation, formalized (with a covariance transformation) by
\citet{garrido2020dealing}. Spline-based surrogates with native factor support
descend from MARS and Bayesian MARS \cite{friedman1991multivariate,
denison1998bayesian}, with BASS as the modern implementation
\cite{francom2020bass}.

\paragraph{Acquisition optimization as a first-class concern.}
That the \emph{inner} optimization matters is established for continuous
domains: \citet{wilson2018maximizing} showed acquisition maximization to
be a critical, understudied component of BO pipelines. In mixed and
discrete spaces, \citet{daulton2022pr} diagnosed the standard
relax-then-round candidate generation as a biased, degenerate
approximation that degrades performance largely independently of the
acquisition function on top, and replaced it with probabilistic
reparameterization; the duplicate-evaluation pathology of rounding was
articulated earlier by \citet{garrido2020dealing} and by
\citet{luong2019discrete}, each fixing it \emph{within} one method (a
transformed kernel; manipulated exploration and length-scale). Our
Sec.~\ref{sec:dedup} treats the same pathology as a protocol-level
property of the comparison rather than a defect of any one model, and
measures its cost across unrelated method families. Closest in spirit to
our attribution concern, \citet{tripp2024diagnosing} showed that fixing
pipeline pitfalls --- including inadequate acquisition maximization ---
makes a basic GP the strongest method on a molecule-design benchmark,
implying prior rankings there reflected pipelines rather than models;
their diagnosis is indirect (a stronger inner optimizer improves
results), where our audit substitutes the true objective to bound the
machinery directly. A ground-truth substitution appears as an internal
ablation in \citet{evogo2025} (a ``real evaluation'' variant estimating
their generative optimizer's ceiling at the training-loss level); we
generalize the idea into a reusable, machinery-isolating diagnostic for
pool-based mixed-space BO.

\paragraph{Benchmarking BO.}
Empirical-foundations critiques have long documented benchmark and
protocol pitfalls \cite{eggensperger2013towards, turner2021bayesian}.
For mixed and combinatorial spaces specifically, the MCBO framework of
\citet{dreczkowski2023mcbo} makes the closest prior argument to ours:
papers proposing a single primitive rarely benchmark against baselines
that hold the remaining primitives fixed, and MCBO supplies shared,
mix-and-match implementations (with Friedman and post-hoc Wilcoxon
testing across combinations). We differ in mechanism and in instrument:
MCBO controls primitives generically, whereas we isolate and measure two
specific acquisition-optimization failure modes (generator restriction
and dedup leakage), quantify them with an oracle-style audit that MCBO
does not perform, and derive per-seed paired statistics gated by that
audit. To our knowledge --- based on a systematic sweep recorded in the
companion repository --- no prior work substitutes the true objective
into the loop as a general machinery audit for mixed-space BO, nor
proposes a machinery-controlled protocol with an oracle gate: method
papers continue to ship bespoke machinery inseparable from the model
(e.g., the trust-region pipeline of \citet{papenmeier2023bounce}), and
benchmark suites standardize tasks and interfaces, but neither audits a
ceiling.

\paragraph{Auditing deployed optimizers.}
A separate tradition audits optimizers as shipped rather than as
described: the black-box optimization challenge of
\citet{turner2021bayesian} evaluated dozens of independently submitted
optimizers under one harness and changed community defaults. Our
ecosystem audit (Sec.~\ref{sec:wild}) belongs to that tradition but
measures a quantity such harnesses do not record: decoded-combination
revisits, counted from inside the objective. The behavior it detects is
partially self-documented --- duplicate suggestions are acknowledged in
the issue trackers of both audited TPE implementations
(\texttt{hyperopt\#608}, which reports 48 identical trials out of 500;
\texttt{optuna\#5440} and \texttt{\#2021}) --- but has not, to our
knowledge, been measured uniformly across libraries, related to the
unavoidable collision baseline, or connected to ranking artifacts.
Positioning against further published audit studies is deferred to a
camera-ready pass with full-text verification.

\section{Setup: One Loop, Shared Machinery}
\label{sec:setup}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Encoding and schema.}
Every input is carried through the loop as a point in the unit cube
$[0,1]^d$. A \emph{schema} declares, per coordinate, whether it is
continuous or categorical with $L_j$ levels. A categorical coordinate
$u \in [0,1]$ decodes to level $k = \min(\lfloor u L_j \rfloor + 1, L_j)$:
the unit interval is split into $L_j$ equal bins, and every point in bin
$k$ is the \emph{same} input to the objective. This encoding leaves
candidate generation, deduplication, and any continuous-relaxation
surrogate untouched, while surrogates with native factor support receive
the decoded levels. The \emph{canonical representation} of a point snaps
each categorical coordinate to its bin centre $(k - \tfrac12)/L_j$, so
that two encodings decoding to the same level combination become exactly
equal --- the representation on which combination-level duplicate
detection operates.

\paragraph{The loop.}
One generic loop drives every method. At each of $B$ iterations it (i)
proposes a pool of $N$ candidates, (ii) scores the pool with the method's
acquisition, (iii) masks candidates whose canonical representation
duplicates an already-evaluated point (tolerance $10^{-10}$), (iv) picks
the arg-max with uniform random tie-breaking, and (v) evaluates the
objective and records it. Random Search replaces (i)--(iv) with uniform
draws rejected against the same duplicate mask. Both surrogate families
use Expected Improvement \cite{jones1998efficient} --- closed-form on the
posterior mean and variance where these are available (GP, random
forest), by Monte Carlo over posterior draws for BASS --- so no method
carries an acquisition-side exploration weight to tune (numerical
settings such as the Monte Carlo draw count remain, fixed across all
comparisons). Runs are paired: all methods
and all machinery variants on a given seed share one space-filling
(maximin Latin hypercube) initial design of $n_0 = \max(2d{+}1, 8)$
points.

\paragraph{The candidate generator and its one degree of freedom.}
The shared generator is a parameter-free hybrid. Half the pool is a fresh
random Latin hypercube over the cube (global exploration; uniform over
level combinations). The other half is local: continuous coordinates are
drawn from a Gaussian cloud centred on the incumbent whose width is the
incumbent's nearest-neighbour distance (clipped to $[10^{-2}, 0.5]$), and
each categorical coordinate independently either keeps the incumbent's
level or flips to a uniformly random other level with probability
$1/n_{\mathrm{cat}}$ --- one flip in expectation, the Hamming-local
notion of ``nearby'' for unordered factors. The degree of freedom this
paper studies is whether a local row may flip \emph{zero} coordinates. In
the \emph{permissive} variant a zero-flip row keeps the incumbent's full
categorical combination and refines only its continuous coordinates ---
on mixed spaces, exactly the exploitation move a model-based method has
over random search. The \emph{restricted} variant forces at least one
flip per local row, so no candidate ever refines the continuous
coordinates at the best-known combination. (On purely categorical
schemas a zero-flip row merely duplicates the incumbent, so one flip is
always forced there and the variants coincide.) Both variants are
standard-looking machinery; nothing in a convergence plot advertises
which one a pipeline uses.

\paragraph{Two implementations, cross-checked where they overlap.}
The pipeline exists twice: the original R library (the thesis pipeline,
with BASS \cite{francom2020bass} and a GP over the continuous
relaxation), which ships the permissive generator, and an independent
Python reimplementation used for the machinery experiments in this paper
(oracle arms, sklearn GP and random forest, Optuna TPE
\cite{akiba2019optuna}), which adds the restricted variant as an
ablation. The Python harness was validated in two stages: first against
pre-registered statistical checks reproducing the R pipeline's recorded
oracle findings; then by a direct R-versus-Python side-by-side on
Func-2C at matched budgets and pool sizes, under different RNG streams
and Latin-hypercube engines, where the permissive generator agrees with
R to five decimals on the attained optimum and a reconstructed
historical restricted generator agrees to about two decimals. The
restricted variant reported in this paper's experiments, and the
remaining benchmarks, ran in Python only. All machinery comparisons hold
the loop, acquisition rule, budget ($B = 80$, $N = 1000$ unless stated),
and per-seed initial designs fixed, so that a machinery axis (generator
variant, dedup level, pool size) is the only moving part.
% TODO: one loop-diagram figure (optional; decide at full-pass review).

\paragraph{The \texttt{bo-audit} wrapper (for libraries we did not build).}
The controlled pipeline above requires owning the loop. To audit
\emph{deployed} libraries (Secs.~\ref{sec:wild}--\ref{sec:equalized}) we
instead wrap the objective function: every optimizer must call it, so a
wrapper that decodes each configuration to its categorical combination
(continuous coordinates rounded to six decimals, far below any
optimizer's meaningful resolution) and counts arrivals at already-seen
keys measures duplicate spending uniformly, with no access to library
internals and no per-library adapters beyond the driver that launches
each tool at its documented defaults. Because collisions are sometimes
unavoidable --- sampling uniformly over $K$ combinations for $B$ steps
collides $B - K\bigl(1-(1-1/K)^{B}\bigr)$ times in expectation --- we
report \emph{excess} revisits over this pigeonhole baseline, so that a
random-sampling control sits at zero in expectation (finite-sample
medians can deviate slightly; Table~\ref{tab:wild}'s Random row shows
$+1$ on the smallest space). The counter was validated before any
library was read --- exact agreement with hand-computed and analytic
ground truth on scripted-repeat, continuous no-false-positive, and
pigeonhole gates, plus detection of the tracker-acknowledged TPE
behavior at the expected order of magnitude --- and a scripted
mixed-space duplicate-injection gate was added and passed before the
equal-budgets re-comparison of Sec.~\ref{sec:equalized}.

\section{The In-the-Wild Audit: Six Deployed Libraries}
\label{sec:wild}
% =============================================================================

We audited six pip-installable BO libraries at their documented defaults
--- Optuna's TPE and GP samplers, Hyperopt's TPE, scikit-optimize's GP,
Ax, and SMAC~3 --- plus a uniform random-sampling control, on six
benchmarks: categorical Ackley at three sizes ($5^3{=}125$,
$5^5{=}3{,}125$, and $11^6{\approx}1.8{\times}10^6$ combinations), the

pre-registered from each library's documented mechanism before the matrix
ran; all seven held, including the null check that the counter registers

\emph{machinery-determined}, splitting the ecosystem into libraries with

the matrix's best categorical solve record while spending a median of 53

distinguishes it from a duplicate-free solver. Second, on the $5^{25}$

propose a duplicate --- a defensible design choice that, like the waste
it avoids, is invisible in convergence curves and surfaced only by the
audit.

\section{Equal Budgets: Does the Waste Change Conclusions?}
\label{sec:equalized}
% =============================================================================

Waste alone does not settle whether machinery distorts
\emph{comparisons}: that requires re-running the comparison with
duplicate handling equalized. Because every benchmark above is
deterministic, a memoization control does this exactly --- duplicate
proposals receive the cached value at zero budget cost, so each
sampler's trajectory is, by construction, bit-identical to its
as-shipped run at the same seed while the meter charges \emph{unique}
evaluations only (80 unique, horizon 400 proposals). We re-ran the two
TPE implementations on all four categorical benchmarks and Optuna's GP
sampler on three (its pest-control row had zero as-shipped revisits, so
memoization is a no-op there and the row carries over) --- 275 runs,
same seeds. The duplicate-free libraries' as-shipped rows likewise carry
over, verified duplicate-free row-by-row for Ax and SMAC, with one
disclosed exception: scikit-optimize's carried $5^3$ row reflects
${\sim}60$ median unique evaluations rather than 80 (its as-shipped
revisits there track the pigeonhole baseline), which cannot change that
cell's outcome since it already solves 25 of 25.

Under the pre-registered ranking metric (median final best, solve-count
tie-break), \textbf{the apparent library ranking changes on three of the
four benchmarks}. The largest movement is Optuna TPE on the
$5^5$ space: as shipped it looks like a weak categorical optimizer
(median best $16.18$, 7 of 25 solves, below Ax); with its
${\sim}29$ wasted evaluations refunded it reaches the exact optimum on
22 of 25 seeds, reversing the order against Ax and closing most of the
gap to the GP tier (22/25 versus 25/25, though not all of it). On
$5^3$ its solve count rises from 21 to 25 of 25, lifting it past
Hyperopt and SMAC; on $11^6$ the two TPE implementations, tied as
shipped, separate with Hyperopt ahead.
Equal-proposal budgets --- what benchmark tables implicitly charge ---
and equal-unique-evaluation budgets --- what an expensive objective
actually costs --- disagree about who wins.

Two honest complications. The refund converts heterogeneously: Hyperopt's
pre-registered strict-improvement prediction on the $5^5$ space
\emph{failed} (median and solve count unchanged at 3 of 25) --- freeing
its wasted budget does not help, because its sampler does not find better
combinations with the extra evaluations; waste is necessary but not
sufficient for a ranking artifact. And the memoized runs expose a second
mechanism: on the 125-combination space, Optuna's GP sampler exhausted
the entire 400-proposal horizon while producing a median of only 30
unique configurations --- five times the proposals bought three more
unique points than its as-shipped 27 --- so its duplicates are not
occasional collisions but the proposal generator's steady state, which
no budget increase can fix. The controlled study that follows isolates
both mechanisms --- generator restriction and duplicate leakage --- in a
pipeline where every part can be held fixed.

\section{The Oracle-Ceiling Audit and Failure Mode I:\\ the Generator Ceiling}
\label{sec:oracle}
% =============================================================================

\begin{definition}[Oracle-ceiling audit]
\label{def:oracle}
Fix a pool-based BO pipeline: a candidate generator $G$, an admissibility
rule $A$ (duplicate handling), a budget $B$, and an initial design. The
\emph{oracle-ceiling audit} replaces the acquisition score of every
candidate $x$ by the true objective, $\alpha(x) = -f(x)$, and runs the
otherwise unchanged loop: at each iteration the best admissible candidate
in the pool is evaluated. The resulting best-so-far curve is the
\emph{machinery ceiling} of $(G, A, B)$.
\end{definition}

Two remarks fix the ceiling's exact epistemic status. Per realized pool,
the oracle's pick is optimal by construction: no acquisition, however
informed, can select a better admissible point from the same pool.
Trajectory-wise, however, the guarantee is not a theorem, because the
pools themselves are history-dependent --- the generator's local half is
centred on the current incumbent, so an oracle run and a surrogate run
diverge in pool content after their first differing pick, and on a
multimodal objective a differently-centred local cloud could in
principle expose points the oracle's own trajectory never generates. The
ceiling should therefore be read as the performance of omniscient
selection \emph{under the pipeline's own pool process}: a bound that is
exact per iteration, and that empirically was never exceeded by any
surrogate arm in any of the several thousand runs behind this paper. We
use it as a diagnostic yardstick, not as a proved envelope.

The audit (Definition~\ref{def:oracle}) has four properties that make it
cheap enough to be a standard
preliminary. It fits no surrogate --- its cost is candidate generation
plus objective evaluations of the pool, which on synthetic or
cheap-to-simulate objectives is negligible (the complete
generator~$\times$~dedup~$\times$~pool-size matrix reported below, 700
runs at budget 80, completes in about five minutes on one core). It is
deterministic given seeds. It is A/B-able: running it once per machinery
variant isolates the machinery axis with no model in the loop. And it
pairs: all variants share per-seed initial designs, so differences are
per-seed comparable.

\paragraph{The generator ceiling, measured.}
Table~\ref{tab:oracle} reports the audit on the two mixed CoCaBO
benchmarks at three pool sizes. The permissive generator attains the
optimum to within numerical precision (per-seed residual
$\le 3\times10^{-5}$ even at $N{=}50$; the optima are computed per
level-combination and verified analytically:
$-0.206326$ for Func-2C at combination $(2,2)$, $-0.722140$ for Func-3C
at $(2,2,1)$) at every pool size tested. The restricted generator loses
on \emph{every one of 25 paired seeds in every cell} (Wilcoxon
signed-rank $p = 6.0\times10^{-8}$, the $n{=}25$ floor), and its
shortfall grows as the pool shrinks --- by a factor of about $14$ on
Func-2C and about $8$ on Func-3C from $N{=}1000$ to $N{=}50$. The
practical reading: with a large pool the global half eventually stumbles
into the optimum basin and the restriction costs little in final value;
with the small pools typical of expensive problems, forbidding continuous
refinement at the incumbent's combination caps even a perfect surrogate
well short of the optimum. Whether the cap binds a \emph{real} surrogate
depends on how close it runs to the ceiling --- our surrogate-level
experiments detected no generator effect for weak (sklearn-grade) GP and
random-forest surrogates at this budget, a point Sec.~\ref{sec:conseq}
treats in full. On the purely categorical benchmarks (permuted-categorical Ackley,
``Cat-Ackley'', used at three sizes throughout; Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments})
the two variants coincide by construction (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}) and the
oracle clears the pool on every seed even at $d{=}6$, $L{=}11$: there the
pool is not the binding constraint, and failures must be attributed
elsewhere.

\section{Failure Mode II: Duplicate Leakage\\ at the Combination Level}
% =============================================================================

\label{sec:dedup}

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
Duplicate handling that compares \emph{raw encodings} treats two points
that decode to the same categorical combination as distinct. The
surrogate's apparent uncertainty lives in encoding space: a fresh
encoding of an already-evaluated combination is a new point in
$[0,1]^d$, so its posterior variance --- and hence its Expected
Improvement --- does not vanish there, even for a surrogate that
interpolates its training points exactly. The acquisition therefore
keeps nominating encodings of combinations the loop has already
measured, and on a deterministic objective every such pick is a wasted
evaluation. Deduplicating on the canonical representation
(Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}) closes the leak by construction.

\begin{figure}[t]
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig_dedup_audit}
\caption{\label{fig:dedup}The leak the convergence plot cannot see.
(a)~Mean best-so-far of the oracle on Cat-Ackley ($d{=}3$, $L{=}5$),
combination-level versus encoding-level dedup, 25 paired seeds: the two
curves are exactly superimposed. (b)~The audit's revisit counts for the
same runs: encoding-level dedup re-spends a median 78 of 80 evaluations
(one dot per seed; bars are medians).}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{At the ceiling and below it.}
Under the oracle the leak is maximally silent
(Fig.~\ref{fig:dedup}): on Cat-Ackley with $125$
combinations the oracle re-picks its already-found optimum for a median
of $78$ of $80$ iterations under encoding-level dedup, while no
benchmark's mean final value moves by even two paired standard errors in
either generator variant --- the convergence plot literally cannot see
it. For real surrogates the same leak is far from harmless
(Table~\ref{tab:dedup}): a GP and a random forest each re-spend $52$--$55$
of $80$ picks on known combinations and drop from solving the benchmark
on every seed to missing it on $2$--$5$ of $25$, with mean finals
degrading toward Random Search. The leak is also not an artifact of one
implementation: Optuna's stock TPE --- its own sampler, no shared pool
--- revisits a median of $53$ of $80$ combinations (mean $52.7$,
Table~\ref{tab:dedup}) on the same benchmark and lands at Random-level
performance. (This TPE arm starts from Optuna's own random startup
rather than the shared design, so its performance figure is
configuration-conditional; the revisit count is the robust part of the
observation.) The thesis
pipeline's pre-fix instrumented BASS runs lost $25$--$29$ of $40$ picks
the same way. Roughly two thirds of the evaluation budget --- across four methods
spanning three unrelated machinery families (the shared pool loop under
GP and RF, TPE's own sampler, and the thesis's BASS pipeline) --- spent
silently re-measuring known points,
while each method still ``ran'' and produced plausible-looking curves.

\section{Consequences for Surrogate Comparisons}
\label{sec:conseq}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Repairing the machinery changes what a comparison means, not
necessarily who wins.}
An early, preliminary reading of our own pipeline suggested that fixing
the candidate generator would flip the spline surrogate's standing
against Random Search on the mixed benchmarks. The full 25-seed protocol
run said otherwise, and we report it as the negative result it is
(Table~\ref{tab:thesis}): with the machinery repaired, shared, and
audited, BASS-BO still \emph{loses} to Random Search on both mixed
benchmarks in per-seed wins, while GP-BO and TPE beat Random comfortably
\emph{through the identical loop, acquisition rule, dedup, and initial
designs}. That is precisely the point of the exercise. Before the
repair, BASS-BO's mixed-space failure was unattributable, on two
documented grounds: the pre-repair library's generator demonstrably
forced a categorical flip on every local candidate (the configuration is
recorded in the pipeline's version history, and the contemporaneous
oracle A/B against that library lost every paired seed --- a result the
final-protocol audit of Sec.~\ref{sec:oracle} re-establishes
independently), and two thirds of the categorical budget was leaking to
revisits (Sec.~\ref{sec:dedup}). After the repair, with the oracle
bounding what the pool permits and the leak closed, the failure
localizes cleanly to the surrogate--acquisition combination itself. A comparison
that once measured machinery now measures models; the ranking did not
flip, but its meaning did.

\caption{\label{tab:thesis}The thesis pipeline's final protocol run
(repaired, shared machinery; 25 paired seeds, budget 80): per-seed
wins/ties/losses against Random Search and paired Wilcoxon $p$.}
\begin{ruledtabular}
\begin{tabular}{llrr}
benchmark & method & W/T/L & $p$ \\
\colrule
Func-2C & GP-BO   & 20/1/4  & $5.2\times10^{-4}$ \\
Func-2C & TPE     & 18/1/6  & $0.0053$ \\
Func-2C & BASS-BO & 5/2/18  & $0.043$ \\

Func-3C & BASS-BO & 8/3/14  & $0.048$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{ruledtabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Where the surrogate does win, the machinery decides whether
that is visible.}
On the solvable purely categorical instance (Cat-Ackley, $d{=}3$,
$L{=}5$), BASS-BO and GP-BO both reach the exact optimum on every seed
(mean and standard deviation numerically zero) under the repaired
machinery --- while under encoding-level dedup a GP of the same class
(the Python-implementation GP-EI of Table~\ref{tab:dedup}, not the
thesis's GP-BO) misses it on a fifth of the seeds. The same
benchmark also illustrates why solvable-in-budget instances are needed
at all: Random Search finds the optimum often enough that the paired
comparison is mostly ties (8/17/0 for both surrogates,
$p = 0.006$), so capability shows in the tie structure, not the win
count.

\paragraph{The generator axis, for real surrogates, at this budget.}
Running sklearn-grade GP and random-forest surrogates through the same
four machinery cells detects \emph{no} permissive-versus-restricted effect on either
mixed benchmark (10--14 wins of 25, all $p \ge 0.09$): both run far
below the oracle ceiling, where their own guidance quality --- not the
pool --- is the binding constraint. A guidance-dial experiment makes
this quantitative within a fixed search type: corrupting the oracle's
score with noise $\sigma\varepsilon$, generator sensitivity decays from
total (25/25 paired wins at $\sigma{=}0$) to statistically undetectable
at the pre-named noise level where attained performance matches a
surrogate of this grade (all four benchmark--pool cells,
$p \ge 0.13$)\footnote{Two disclosures attach to this finding. An
earlier run of the same dial on different seeds returned an inconclusive
composite verdict: its win-rate clauses matched the pattern reported
here in all four cells, but a gap-decay clause --- diagnosed post hoc as
mis-specified at near-ceiling cells --- failed in two. The present
result is a pre-registered, fresh-seed retest of the win-rate criterion
only, with a no-further-retests stopping rule; criteria were fixed and
committed before the run. The decay is also not monotone: one
small-pool cell retains a nominally significant residual effect
($18/25$, $p = 0.004$) at the largest noise level, so ``undetectable''
means at the pre-named anchor, not at every noise level beyond it.}.
The oracle audit is what makes this regime legible: the ceiling tells
you what the machinery permits; the surrogate's distance from it tells
you whether machinery choices can matter for that surrogate at all.

\paragraph{What remains surrogate-attributable.}
With the machinery axes controlled, the residual differences are the
model's. The companion diagnostics
(\texttt{3\_categorical\_diagnostics} in the companion repository)
measured held-out rank quality of the spline surrogate at BO sample
sizes, in a diagnostic run that predates the machinery repair: moderate
on the mixed benchmarks, and on the hardest categorical instance a
correlation whose near-intercept fits carry little usable signal at
thesis budgets. (Fit quality is measured on random designs, independent
of the machinery axes later repaired, so the measurement plausibly
carries over; it was not re-taken post-repair.) This is consistent with
the final rankings above, and with the thesis's conclusion that the
benchmark scale, not only the model, determines what a budget can
reveal.

\section{A Protocol for Machinery-Controlled Comparisons}
% =============================================================================

\label{sec:protocol}

None of the individual ingredients below is new; each has precedents in
the methods literature (SMAC's one-exchange local moves
\cite{hutter2011sequential}, Casmopolitan's Hamming-local trust regions
\cite{wan2021think}, shared modular primitives in benchmarking
frameworks). The protocol's content is their \emph{composition as a
control}, imposed identically on every method being compared, plus the
audit that verifies the control worked.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Share one schema-aware candidate generator.}
  \emph{Rationale:} the generator sets a ceiling on every method
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:oracle}); if it differs between methods, the comparison
  confounds machinery with model. \emph{Cost:} an implementation pass;
  the moves themselves are standard.
  \item \textbf{Deduplicate at the decoded-combination level.}
  \emph{Rationale:} encoding-level dedup silently re-spends the majority
  of a categorical budget and its cost is invisible in convergence
  curves (Sec.~\ref{sec:dedup}). \emph{Cost:} one canonicalization
  function; under other encodings (one-hot, embeddings) the same rule
  applies --- canonicalize to whatever representation the objective
  actually distinguishes before comparing.
  \item \textbf{Run the oracle-ceiling audit per machinery variant,
  before attributing anything to a surrogate.} \emph{Rationale:} it
  isolates the machinery axis with no model in the loop, and it locates
  each surrogate's regime --- a surrogate far below the ceiling is
  guidance-limited, and machinery effects measured on it will be
  attenuated or absent (Sec.~\ref{sec:conseq}). The ceiling distance is
  necessary but not sufficient for attribution: turning it into a
  detectability threshold for a given surrogate class took a separate,
  materially larger calibration in our case (the guidance dial,
  Sec.~\ref{sec:conseq}). \emph{Cost:} minutes on cheap objectives for
  the audit itself; on expensive objectives, run it on a cheap stand-in
  with the same input schema.
  \item \textbf{Pair the seeds and report per-seed win/tie/loss with a
  signed-rank test, not only mean curves.} \emph{Rationale:} mean curves
  completely hid one of this paper's failure modes and understated the
  other; per-seed statistics on shared initial designs surface both at
  $n{=}25$. \emph{Cost:} bookkeeping.
  \item \textbf{Scale benchmark instances so at least one is solvable
  within budget and one is not, and report both.} \emph{Rationale:}
  solvable instances measure capability (and surface tie structure);
  unsolvable ones measure guidance quality; a single scale conflates
  the two (Sec.~\ref{sec:conseq}). \emph{Cost:} one extra instance per
  benchmark family.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{A machinery-disclosure convention.}
Papers comparing surrogates over mixed or categorical spaces could state,
in one table: the candidate-generation scheme (global/local split, local
move distribution, whether local moves can retain the incumbent's
combination), the duplicate-handling representation (raw encoding vs
decoded combination) and tolerance, the pool size per iteration, the
initial-design type and whether it is shared across methods and seeds,
and the machinery ceiling attained by the oracle audit under that
configuration. Every entry is a one-line fact the authors already know;
collectively they are the difference between a surrogate comparison and
a machinery comparison. Table~\ref{tab:disclosure} instantiates the
template for this paper's own pipeline.

\section{Experiments}
% =============================================================================

\label{sec:experiments}

The synthetic benchmarks follow one protocol: budget $80$, $25$ paired
seeds with shared initial designs, per-seed win/tie/loss with Wilcoxon
signed-rank tests. Benchmarks: the CoCaBO mixed functions Func-2C/3C
\cite{ru2020cocabo} and permuted-categorical Ackley at three sizes
($d{=}3/L{=}5$ solvable in budget; $d{=}4/L{=}7$; $d{=}6/L{=}11$ with
$1.77$ million combinations). The thesis pipeline additionally reports
one real tuning task under the same shared machinery at its own scale:
hyperparameter tuning of a small neural text classifier over a fully
categorical eight-dimensional space (budget $60$, $25$ seeds --- each
evaluation is a real training run). There, BASS-BO and GP-BO both beat
Random Search in paired wins ($17/3/5$, $p = 0.025$, and $16/2/7$,
$p = 0.024$, respectively) while TPE does not ($12/2/11$, $p = 0.96$) ---
the categorical-capability pattern of the synthetic benchmarks carrying
over. (An earlier elastic-net candidate was run but dropped by the
thesis as uninformative --- its models converged almost immediately,
leaving nothing for an optimizer to find; its artifacts remain in the
companion repository.) All machinery \emph{ablations} in this paper,
however, are synthetic-benchmark results; no ablation was run on a real
task.

The completed matrix comprises: (i) the oracle-ceiling audit over
generator $\times$ dedup $\times$ pool size
(Sec.~\ref{sec:oracle}--\ref{sec:dedup}; 700 runs); (ii) the surrogate
$\times$ machinery matrix with sklearn GP-EI and RF-EI through all four
machinery cells, plus stock TPE and Random references (750 runs;
Sec.~\ref{sec:conseq}); (iii) the guidance-dial experiment and its
pre-registered fresh-seed retest ($2450$ runs; Sec.~\ref{sec:conseq});
and (iv) the thesis pipeline's final protocol run with BASS-BO, GP-BO,
TPE, and Random on all benchmarks (Table~\ref{tab:thesis}). Every
experiment carried a pre-registered design --- hypotheses and decision
criteria written before the runs, and for the later experiments
committed to version control before the results existed, which is
independently verifiable from the repository history --- and every
experiment was independently re-verified against its raw results; two
pre-registered hypotheses failed and are reported as such
(Secs.~\ref{sec:dedup} and~\ref{sec:conseq}).

One planned cell remains open: BASS itself through the restricted/leaky
machinery ablations. BASS's R implementation was not runnable in the
environment where the Python matrix was executed; the companion
repository ships the exact commands
(\texttt{run\_all\_final.sh} and the diagnostics harness under
\texttt{3\_categorical\_diagnostics}), and the pre-fix instrumented
BASS runs already measure the dedup leak for BASS at smaller scale
($25$--$29$ of $40$ picks). We state this as an open cell rather than
extrapolating the Python results to BASS.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{What the audit can and cannot say.}
The oracle-ceiling audit bounds machinery, not model quality: it can
exonerate or indict the pool that all methods share, and it can locate a
surrogate's regime relative to the ceiling, but it cannot rank
surrogates. Its trajectory-level guarantee is empirical, not proved
(Sec.~\ref{sec:oracle}): the pools are history-dependent, and a
differently-guided run explores differently-centred pools. Our
guidance-degradation evidence has a further scope limit: the dial
corrupts the oracle with independent noise, which is one degradation
model; a \emph{biased} surrogate --- one that is systematically wrong in
a structured way, for example by over-smoothing --- degrades guidance
differently, and its interaction with the generator restriction is
untested. The objection this invites should be answered head on: does
the generator confound matter for real methods, or only for oracles? On
the evidence here, no real surrogate we ran was measurably affected by
the generator restriction --- the observational case for its practical
relevance is inferential, resting on the dial calibration (sensitivity
decays with guidance quality within a fixed search type) plus the
observation that better-guided methods than ours exist. The dedup
confound needs no such inference: it taxed every method we ran,
directly. A comparison paper adopting our protocol gets the generator
control nearly for free either way; the audit is what tells its authors
whether that control was load-bearing for their methods.

\paragraph{Scope of the machinery family.}
Everything here concerns pool-based acquisition optimization: propose,
score, mask, pick. Gradient-based acquisition maximization over
continuous relaxations and trust-region schemes need adapted audits ---
the natural analogues (an oracle inside the trust region; the true
objective as the function being maximized by the inner optimizer) are
straightforward to state but were not evaluated here. Likewise, the
dedup argument assumes a deterministic objective, where every revisit is
pure waste; under observation noise, revisiting is legitimate
replication, and duplicate handling becomes a design choice about
replication policy rather than a leak. A further scope limit is
dimensional and empirical: every machinery ablation in this paper runs
on low-dimensional synthetic benchmarks (two to six categorical
coordinates, at most two continuous); the real-task evidence comes from
the thesis pipeline only and was not subjected to the ablations.
Higher-dimensional spaces, where candidate pools cover vanishing
fractions of the domain, could plausibly make the generator restriction
bind harder --- that is conjecture, stated as such.

\paragraph{Open cells and verification limits.}
The spline surrogate itself has not been run through the restricted and
leaky machinery ablations (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}); we deliberately
do not extrapolate the Python-surrogate results to it. Our novelty
statement rests on a recorded 39-source sweep in which the three closest
works were verified at the abstract and companion-code level rather than
full text (an environment constraint); the sweep and its per-source
assessments are versioned in the companion repository so the check can
be extended. Finally, one methodological debt is disclosed rather than
hidden: two of this paper's experiment analyses were refuted in internal
adversarial review and corrected before inclusion --- the surrogate-matrix
analysis, for reframing a failed generator hypothesis as a new supported
finding beyond its pre-registered fallback, and the first guidance-dial
analysis, for declaring a TPE configuration question closed when its
pre-registered joint criterion had been missed by one seed --- alongside
several section drafts corrected for similar overclaims. The review
records are versioned with the experiments.

\paragraph{Relation to reproducibility critiques.}
Our findings are a mixed-space instance of a broader pattern documented
by benchmarking critiques \cite{eggensperger2013towards,
turner2021bayesian, dreczkowski2023mcbo}: apparent method differences
often live in uncontrolled parts of the pipeline. The machinery axes we
isolate are unusually easy to control --- a shared generator is an
implementation choice, combination-level dedup is one function, and the
audit that verifies both costs minutes --- which is what makes a
protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}), rather than a lament, possible. Two statistical notes: we apply no multiple-comparison
correction across the many cells reported --- the load-bearing results
(25/25 sweeps at the Wilcoxon floor) survive any correction, and
descriptive non-anchor $p$-values are labeled as such where they appear
--- and ``statistically undetectable'' is used throughout in the plain
sense of failing a pre-named test at $\alpha=0.05$, not as evidence of
exact absence.
