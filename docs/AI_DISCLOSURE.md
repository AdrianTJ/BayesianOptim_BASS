# AI use in this thesis

This document records where and how AI tooling was used in the production of
this thesis and its accompanying codebase. It is the source material for the
disclosure section to be added to the thesis after review. The guiding
principle throughout was that the author directs, decides, and approves;
the AI assistant drafts, implements, and verifies under that direction.

## Tools

Anthropic's Claude Code (CLI and desktop app), in interactive sessions driven
by the author, using Claude models (Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.8, and Fable 5 across
the project's lifetime). Sessions worked on dedicated branches (the historical
`claude/*` branch names) and every change reached `main` through a pull
request reviewed by the author. No AI-generated content entered the thesis or
codebase without passing through that review.

## Where AI was used

### 1. Code and infrastructure (with AI assistance)

The benchmarking pipeline in `code_files/` was developed iteratively with
Claude Code, on top of the author's original optimization code:

- Restructure into a shared R library and the Expected Improvement redesign
  (PR #4).
- Schema-aware candidate generation for categorical inputs (PR #10) and the
  categorical bottleneck fixes: keep-combination local moves,
  combination-level duplicate handling, the three-size Cat-Ackley protocol,
  and paired statistics (PR #11).
- The TPE (Optuna) baseline integration (PR #7) and the TPE
  gamma-sensitivity ablation (PR #8).
- Runner and collection scripts (`run_all_final.sh`,
  `collect_thesis_artifacts.sh`, `run_on_ec2.sh`), the diagnostics suite
  (`code_files/3_categorical_diagnostics/`), and the R test suite.

### 2. Experiments (author-executed)

The final benchmark runs (budget 80, seeds 1001 to 1025) were executed by the
author on his own EC2 instance using the scripts above. The outputs were
committed verbatim to `final_results/`; the commit history of that directory
is the audit trail. AI did not generate, select, or filter any experimental
result.

### 3. Thesis text (AI-drafted, author-reviewed, visibly marked)

The theory chapters (Gaussian Processes, Bayesian Optimization, SMBO,
Surrogate Models, and the core of the BASS chapter) are the author's original
writing. AI-assisted edits to the thesis were made in two passes and are
individually visible: every passage added or modified in these passes is
wrapped in the `\green{}` macro and renders green in the draft under review.

- Pass 1 (PR #11, PR #12): consistency edits made before the final results
  existed. Protocol descriptions were matched to the implemented pipeline,
  and every claim that ran ahead of the data was rewritten in conditional
  form.
- Pass 2 (PR #12): insertion of the measured results (figures, tables,
  numbers), the Result Interpretation and Limitations section, and the
  rewriting of the Introduction and Conclusions from conditional phrasing to
  measured outcomes.

### 4. Verification and honesty auditing

Before Pass 2, Claude Code independently cross-checked every results-related
claim in the thesis against the raw CSVs in `final_results/`, recomputing the
paired statistics from the per-seed data. This audit produced corrections
that were applied in Pass 2, all in the direction of weaker claims:

- The mixed-benchmark outcome (BASS-BO losing to Random Search on Func-2C/3C)
  is reported as a failure, with the same prominence as the positive results.
- The claim of competitiveness with TPE was restricted to the purely
  categorical benchmarks, where the data supports it.
- A benchmark that had run in the protocol but gone unreported (the synthetic
  non-smooth surface) was added to the thesis.

### 5. Figures

Convergence figures are generated by the pipeline's plotting code from the
committed CSVs. Three theory-chapter figures were regenerated from the
scripts in `code_files/figure_generations/`.

### 6. Copy editing

A dedicated style pass (PR #9 and the final pass in PR #12) enforced the
author's writing rules (punctuation and word choices) across all chapters.

## What was not AI

The research question, the choice of BASS as a surrogate, the experimental
design decisions (benchmark scope, the three-size Cat-Ackley protocol, the
exclusion of the Elastic Net case study), the interpretation stance, all
advisor interaction, and the final approval of every line of text and code
are the author's.

## Audit trail

- Pull requests #4 through #13 on the project repository document each
  AI-assisted work package and its review.
- The `\green{}` markup in the thesis draft marks every AI-era text edit
  until the final version turns the markup black.
- `final_results/` commit history ties every number in the thesis to a
  specific benchmark run.
